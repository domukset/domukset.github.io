5.8.2026 New Break-in & Old Fingerprints

Once again, the sauna door has been vandalized in an apparent attempt to gain entry to the premises, an incident that comes as little surprise given that little action has been taken to improve building security. In a related matter, we confirmed today that former residents still have active fingerprint access to the gym and, by extension, the building itself. Despite the housing company being notified of this issue on 13.7, the problem remains unresolved. Compounding the issue, residents from nearby buildings who hold valid gym credentials are oblivious to the ongoing security breaches—and thus, are not on guard against outsiders trying to gain entry through their access.

23.7.2026 Aamulehti's article is published

https://www.aamulehti.fi/tampere/art-2000012159369.html

Comments from the Aamulehti article:

Ten years ago, when I lived in a TOAS apartment, a drug user who was staying in the building broke into all the storage lockers and stole bicycles from the bike storage area. He was caught red-handed many times and had, among other things, taken items he’d pilfered from other people’s lockers and put them in his own. Numerous criminal reports were filed with the police and complaints were made to TOAS. The police did nothing, and TOAS stated that this kind of thing is just part of living in a TOAS apartment and you just have to put up with it. Nothing seems to have changed in the past ten years when it comes to living in a TOAS apartment.

No one is really interested in setting things up so that a break-in isn’t possible or would be as difficult as possible, because… The maintenance company will have extra billable work as a result of the incident. The cleaning company will have extra billable work. The locksmith company will have extra billable work. The property management company will have extra billable work. So why stop a good money-maker when, in the end, the tenant is the one paying for it all? It’s important, however, to empathize with the residents’ distress and at least appear to be doing something. “You’ve filed a police report, right? You have home insurance, don’t you? And these things happen all the time, don’t they?” These are important phrases that express sympathy for what’s happened, even though we know they don’t offer much comfort. You can get inside through the combination locks on the front doors with a little effort, etc. There are numerous ways to disable an electric lock on a front door so that it no longer works. You can break into the metal doors of storage units in a minute with a crowbar, since the doors and frames are made of sheet metal. If there’s enough clearance and the latch doesn’t have a deadbolt, you can get inside almost without leaving a trace. And this charade just goes on and on, and nobody really cares—everyone benefits, well, except for the victim of the break-in, but who really cares when these things just keep happening...?

TOAS could stop skimping on this issue and install surveillance cameras instead of relying on residents to keep an eye on things. Furthermore, the fact that the police don’t dare to go out and catch thieves in the act is pathetic—it’s as if they want to let the thieves get away. They certainly have enough courage for traffic enforcement, though—against taxpayers.

13.7.2026 CLOSING OF THE COMMON ROOM

NOTICE: After finding out there was a party on 29.6 that ended with police presence because party goers climbed on the roof top of the building, The Housing Company decides to close the Club Room to all residents citing vandalism in the premises, which was NOT caused by the party.

Following community discussions, the Housing Company has been informed that residents fail to see the connection between the closure of the club room and the security issues we have been experiencing in the building. The root of the problem lies elsewhere—namely, in uncontrolled building access through the main entrances and the outdated fingerprint system that still grants entry to former residents and outsiders. The club room was never the issue. As a result, many residents feel unfairly penalized by the Housing Company's unilateral decision. They are now asking for a clear explanation and, more importantly, a concrete solution to restore access as soon as possible. We would like to formally request that the club room be equipped with the One4all access system, which would allow controlled and traceable entry for authorized tenants only.
